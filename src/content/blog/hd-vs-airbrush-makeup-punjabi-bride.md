Every second enquiry we get asks the same question: "Do you do airbrush?" Yes — and HD. But the better question is "which one is right for me?" Here is exactly how we think about it.

## What HD makeup actually is

HD makeup is applied by hand with high-definition products: finely milled foundations and powders designed not to reflect light or show texture on a high-resolution camera. It is buildable, so we can go sheer on good skin and fuller where needed. It is the default for most brides and the kinder choice for dry, mature or textured skin.

## What airbrush makeup actually is

Airbrush makeup is sprayed on as a fine mist through a compressor and a stylus. The result is an even, lightweight layer that sits on the skin rather than in it. It is excellent for long, hot days, for oily skin, and for brides who hate the feeling of foundation. It is less forgiving on very dry skin and harder to spot-correct once set.

## The honest comparison

| | HD makeup | Airbrush makeup |
|---|---|---|
| Finish | Natural, buildable | Ultra-light, uniform |
| Wear | 10–14 hours | 12–16 hours |
| Dry / winter skin | Excellent | Needs a hydrating formula |
| Oily skin / heat | Good with prep | Excellent |
| Spot correction on the day | Easy | Trickier once set |
| Photographs | Beautifully | Beautifully |

## What decides it in Punjab

**The month.** December fog and 8 °C mornings dry skin out — HD or a hydrating airbrush formula. May and June heat with an outdoor sangeet — airbrush.

**The length of the day.** A 5 am Anand Karaj and a 10 pm reception is a sixteen-hour day. Airbrush, or HD with a standby artist for a midday refresh.

**Your skin on the trial day.** We look at your skin under studio lights and with a flash. Texture, dryness, oil and how it takes to primer tell us more than any rule.

**How you like to feel.** Some brides want to feel the base is there; others want to forget it. Both are valid.

## What matters more than either

Skin prep (a week of hydration, no new products), colour-matching to the neck, flash-testing at the trial, and proper setting. Get those right and both techniques look flawless at the doli. Get them wrong and neither saves you.
