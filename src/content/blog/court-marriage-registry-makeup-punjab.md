Not every Punjabi wedding runs the full roka-to-reception calendar. A growing number of couples — sometimes marrying before the families finish organising the bigger functions, sometimes by choice, sometimes because of visa timelines for an NRI partner — register their marriage in court first. If that's you, the makeup brief should look different from a full bridal booking, and most of the confusion we hear on WhatsApp is because nobody tells brides that clearly enough.

## Why the brief changes

A full wedding-day look is built to survive 10–12 hours under mandap lights, camera flashes, pheras, and a change of outfit for the reception. A court marriage is usually a 30–60 minute appointment, often in daylight, with one outfit and far less physical movement. Building a full bridal face for that — heavy contour, false lashes sized for stage photography, hairstyling meant to carry a dupatta and jewellery for half a day — is simply more than the occasion needs. It also takes longer and costs more than it should.

Tell your artist upfront: "This is for a court marriage, not the wedding day." A good artist will scale the whole booking down — time, products, and price — not just do a lighter version of the same long appointment.

## What a lighter brief actually includes

- **Skin prep and base** — still full attention here; your skin is the part of the look that should never get rushed, even for a 30-minute ceremony.
- **Soft-glam eyes** — a wash of shimmer or a neutral smoky shade, skipping heavy cut-crease work that needs 45 minutes on its own.
- **Natural lashes or a light strip** — enough definition for photos without the volume built for stage lighting.
- **A everyday-appropriate lip** — nudes, soft corals, or a muted rose rather than the deep reds many brides wear for the main wedding day.
- **Simple hair** — an open blow-dry with soft waves, a low bun, or a half-tied style. Most registry brides skip the heavy braided or jewelled updos entirely.

This is close to the soft-glam approach we'd suggest for an [engagement look](/blog/engagement-makeup-soft-glam-ring-photos/) or a simple family function — see our [packages](/packages/) for how multi-event bookings are typically structured, since a registry appointment often sits alongside a trial or another small function in the same visit.

## Timing that actually fits a registry morning

Court appointments usually have a fixed time slot, and being late can mean rebooking a date. Build in a buffer:

- **60–90 minutes** for makeup and simple hair, door to door.
- Book your artist to arrive **at least 30 minutes before** you need to leave, not exactly when you need to leave.
- If the ceremony is in [Jalandhar](/areas/jalandhar/), [Chandigarh](/areas/chandigarh/), [Ludhiana](/areas/ludhiana/) or another Punjab city with unpredictable traffic, confirm your artist's own travel time separately from your getting-ready time.

Compare this to the [full wedding-morning timeline](/blog/punjabi-wedding-makeup-timeline-roka-to-reception/) most brides follow — a registry day borrows almost none of that structure, and that's by design.

## What to wear

A heavy bridal lehenga borrowed for "just the court day" usually works against you — it photographs as half a bridal look rather than a complete one. A well-chosen saree, a simple suit, or a lighter lehenga in a colour you'd wear again tends to look more intentional on camera. Solid jewel tones (deep teal, maroon, mustard, dusty pink) photograph cleanly with soft-glam makeup; save the heavier embroidery and the full jewellery set for the functions still to come.

## If the full wedding is still ahead

Many couples who register first still have a complete Punjabi wedding — mehndi, Anand Karaj or pheras, reception — planned for weeks or months later. If that's your situation, say so when you book the court-marriage appointment. It helps your artist plan two separate briefs instead of one in-between compromise, and it's often easier to lock in your date and trial for the main event at the same time. ## A quieter kind of beautiful

A registry morning doesn't need less care — it needs a different brief. Skin that looks genuinely good in daylight, a natural lash, hair that won't need re-pinning in a government office waiting room, and a lip you'd actually wear on an ordinary Tuesday. That's the goal, and it's a booking worth being specific about.
